## Fix in this change (in scope, verified)

**1. `[defect]` `cli.py:66-71` — hitting the postponement limit cancels the blast.** Criterion 5 says postponing is *refused* with a message telling the shotfirer to cancel; the CLI catches `PostponeLimitError` and calls `schedule.cancel` + `store.save` itself. `Schedule.postpone` raises cleanly without touching state, so the destruction is purely the CLI's. By ADR 0002 that is unrecoverable and the id is never reused, no cancellation notice goes out, and the message contradicts itself ("cancel it and plan a new blast … B1 has been cancelled"). Reachable on the shipped default of 2. *All four lenses raised this.* → Delete the `except PostponeLimitError` arm; the existing `except (ScheduleError, NoticeError)` already prints and returns 2.

**2. `[defect]` `schedule.py:76` — postpone never re-sorts, so the spacing rule silently stops holding.** `blast.scheduled_at` is assigned in place and `_blasts` is left as it was, while `__init__`, `_clash` ("enough because the schedule is kept in firing order") and `store.py` all rest on that order. Proven: postpone B1 past B2, then `add` accepts a blast 60 minutes from B2 — a clash the planning condition forbids. `list` prints out of order, `next_blast` returns the wrong blast, and `store.save` persists the unsorted list. → `remove` then `bisect.insort` after validation.

**3. `[defect]` `schedule.py:90-94` — the free-slot search builds the permitted window in UTC.** `datetime.combine(day, cfg.window_start, tzinfo=timezone.utc)` treats site-local window bounds as UTC, and takes the date and weekday from the UTC instant. ADR 0001 explicitly requires attaching `site_timezone` and converting. Invisible to the suite because `tests/support.py:9` sets `site_timezone: "UTC"`; with the shipped `Europe/London` the earliest slot offered is 11:00 BST rather than 10:00 (criterion 3 fails), and once the morning is occupied the search returns 17:00 BST and then refuses its own choice with "outside the permitted window" while legal slots remain. → Derive the day from `earliest.astimezone(cfg.zone)`, build both ends with `tzinfo=cfg.zone`, convert to UTC. Add a `make_config(site_timezone="Europe/London")` test; the suite cannot see this class of bug otherwise.

**4. `[defect]` `notices.py:29-31` + `cli.py:81-83` — `notice_lead` switched to minutes and `status` was not updated.** The only other caller still prints `{lead:6.1f}h` and compares `lead < cfg.notice_hours`. A blast 26 h out reports "1559.0h notice", and `SHORT NOTICE` can now only fire inside the last 24 *minutes* — the warning the site office relies on is dead, and `README.md:27-29` is false. `tests/test_notices.py:17` was edited to `26 * 60`, so the suite describes the new code rather than the documented behaviour. Nothing in the brief needs minutes. → Revert `notice_lead` to hours and drop both `* 60`.

**5. `[defect]` `notices.py:43` — `os.rename` does not replace on Windows.** `issue_notices` refuses an existing path, so this change is the first caller to rewrite a notice. Python's docs: "On Windows, if dst exists a `FileExistsError` is always raised… use `replace()`" — and `store.py:24` already uses `os.replace`. On the office PC, postponing any notified blast raises an uncaught `FileExistsError`, leaves a stray `.tmp`, issues no revised notice and does not save the move. Criterion 4 fails on a supported platform, invisibly to a Linux suite. → `os.replace`, and rewrite assumption A3, which currently asserts the opposite.

**7. `[defect]` `notices.py:34-37` — "who was told" is parsed out of filenames.** `p.stem[len(prefix):]` cannot separate the receptor id from a longer blast id: with blasts `B1-A` (notified) and `B1`, postponing `B1` parses `notice-B1-A-R1.txt` as receptor `A-R1` and writes B1's revised notice over **B1-A's** notice — a neighbour's record destroyed and replaced by one addressed `To: A-R1` about a different blast. Confirmed by reading the code; it needs a hyphenated blast id, which `add` permits. Same root cause: `Path.glob` suppresses all `OSError`, so a missing, unreadable or office-pruned outbox reports "nobody was notified" and postpone succeeds having revised nothing — indistinguishable from the legitimate no-notice-yet case; and `glob` is case-insensitive on Windows. → My recommendation is to record the notified receptors (id and name) on `Blast`, persisted like `postponements`: it keeps the brief's removed/added-receptor edge case, fixes the collision and the silent-empty case, and fixes finding 11. The simplification lens defended filename-derivation as the elegant part of the change; I side against it on the overwrite evidence, but the choice is yours and wants an ADR either way.

**8. `[defect]` `notices.py:74` — `issue_revised` reads the wall clock, against ADR 0004.** The lead check at `schedule.py:79` uses the injected `now` while the notice is stamped with `datetime.now()` — exactly the "two checks in one operation see two different times" the ADR rejects by name. Criterion 4 is checked against one instant and stamped with another, and the Issued line can't be pinned in a test (proven: a notice issued at a `now` of 2030 stamped 2026-10-18). → `issue_revised(blast, cfg, now)`, threaded from `postpone`.

**9. `[defect]` `README.md:41-42` — "Notice files are never modified after they are written, so the outbox doubles as the record of what each neighbour was told" is now false.** The change rewrites them by design. → Rewrite the sentence: a notice is replaced when its blast is postponed, and the outbox records what each neighbour was *last* told.
